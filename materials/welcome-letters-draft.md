# Welcome letters: draft updated for the current president

Transcribed from the original materials (`original_paper/independent_materials.pdf` and
`original_paper/interdependent_materials.pdf`). Everything is word for word except the
lines marked **[CHANGED]**, which update facts about the president so the letter is
accurate in 2026. Nothing about the independent/interdependent themes has been changed.

| | Original (2012) | Draft (2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| President | John L. Hennessy, 10th president | Jonathan Levin, 13th president (since August 2024) |
| Faculty tenure line | "a faculty member for 27 years" | "a faculty member for more than 25 years" (on the Stanford faculty since 2000) |
| Signature | Scanned handwritten signature | Typed name only (see open decisions) |
| Header photo | Hennessy headshot | To decide (see open decisions) |

## Open decisions (yours and the instructor's)

1. **Attribution to a real, named person.** Both versions present a letter that the president
   did not write, on a page that will be publicly reachable on GitHub Pages. The original
   study did the same, and the "learning styles" framing is already a cover story, but check
   with the course staff that the approved protocol covers this. The debrief should say plainly
   that the researchers wrote the letter and that it does not come from the president.
2. **Signature and photo.** Reproducing a real signature or an official headshot is a further
   step beyond the original's text. Options: typed name only, a generic header with no photo,
   or the president's official photo if the course staff approve. Whichever you pick, use the
   same choice in both conditions so the letters differ only in their themes.
3. **Campus photos.** The original used different student photos for each condition (solo
   students vs. groups), which is part of the manipulation. Replacement photos need to keep
   that contrast and be images you are allowed to use.
4. **Tenure wording.** "More than 25 years" stays true for the whole data collection period.
   "26 years" is also accurate for 2026.

> # Welcome to Stanford University

> For the next few years, you will have many opportunities to explore new areas of interest and
> to learn from our faculty and from your own individual experiences.
>
> As Stanford's **13th** president and a faculty member for **more than 25 years**, I encourage
> you to make the most of your time here, and I offer you a few suggestions to get you started.
> **[CHANGED: was "10th" and "27 years"]**
>
> Stanford has an accomplished faculty. These teachers and researchers are here because of you
> and your desire to develop your extraordinary talents and unique interests. From your first
> days on campus, I urge you to get to know our faculty. Stop by during office hours to initiate
> a discussion, to share your ideas, or to simply tell the professor what you think about the
> course.
>
> Stanford presents you with many opportunities not available at other institutions. The
> university's breadth—from the arts to the environment to athletics to the sciences—provides
> you with individual freedom to cross departmental boundaries and to discover your
> intellectual passions.
>

> in small classes. If you are interested in research, Stanford offers you numerous avenues for
> pursuing student-initiated research or independent studies.

> journey. Some of your most valuable experiences might involve working independently to
